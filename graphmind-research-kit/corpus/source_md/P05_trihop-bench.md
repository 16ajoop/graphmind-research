# TriHop-Bench: Stress-Testing Retrieval on Three- and Four-Hop Questions

Tomás Ruiz (1), Meenakshi Sundaram (2), Wei Zhang (1)

(1) Instituto Altamira   (2) University of Coromandel

JALS 2023 · Published 2 October 2023

## Abstract

We introduce TriHop-Bench, 6,000 English questions requiring three or four reasoning hops, and evaluate three published retrievers using their released code. All of them lose accuracy as hops increase, but the graph-based GW-QA degrades least. The advantage of graph-based retrieval over dense retrieval grows as the number of hops increases.

Keywords: benchmark, multi-hop retrieval, evaluation

## 1 Introduction

KaveriHop [1] stops at three hops, and older benchmarks such as HotpotQA [5] at two. TriHop-Bench extends evaluation to four hops, where error compounding becomes severe.

## 2 Benchmark

Questions were generated by chaining three or four encyclopedic passages through bridge entities and then rewritten by annotators. The 3-hop subset has 3,600 questions and the 4-hop subset 2,400. We do not release gold entity links for the test set.

## 3 Systems

We evaluate DenseHop [2], GW-QA [3] and HopRank [4], each run with the authors' released code and default settings.

| Method | Source | TriHop-Bench 3-hop EM | TriHop-Bench 4-hop EM |
|---|---|---|---|
| DenseHop | ours | 28.4 | 9.0 |
| GW-QA | ours | 34.7 | 17.5 |
| HopRank | ours | 31.9 | 11.2 |

*Table 1: Exact match on TriHop-Bench.*

## 4 Results

GW-QA leads DenseHop by 6.3 EM on 3-hop and 8.5 EM on 4-hop questions. The advantage of graph-based retrieval over dense retrieval grows as the number of hops increases.

## 5 Conclusion

Four-hop questions remain far from solved: the best system reaches only 17.5 EM.
